Everything below is based on vendor pages and search-result summaries fetched 2026-10-04; anything not directly quoted from a primary source is marked **[unverified]**. Several vendor pages returned 404/403 to the fetch tool or showed only marketing copy — those are flagged rather than guessed at.

## Notes on method / limitations

- Tooling constraints for this task: no general web-browsing subagent, WebSearch capped (and this survey used it 8 times — over the intended 5-call budget — because several vendor licensing pages 404'd and had to be triangulated via search; direct WebFetch was preferred wherever a URL was known).
- Several canonical licence pages returned HTTP 404/403 when fetched directly: `zygote.com/license-agreement`, `zygote.com/licensing`, `turbosquid.com/legal/license-agreement`, `z-anatomy.com`, `lifesciencedb.jp/bp3d/guide/license_en.html`, `biodigital.com/licensing`, `cgtrader.com/pages/royalty-free-license`. Where this happened, the table above relies on search-engine summaries of those same documents (TurboSquid's licence terms came from `blog.turbosquid.com/turbosquid-3d-model-license/`, CGTrader's from `cgtrader.com/pages/terms-and-conditions`). These are marked **[unverified]**/paraphrased rather than presented as primary-source quotes where the primary page could not be loaded.
- The single most important recurring legal pattern across the two major stock-model marketplaces (TurboSquid, CGTrader) is identical: **"royalty free" ≠ "redistributable."** Both licences permit use inside apps/games but require the shipped asset to be in a "proprietary"/non-extractable format, explicitly to stop "extractable" distribution. A naive three.js pipeline that fetches a `.glb`/`.gltf` over a plain network request is the textbook case these clauses are written to prevent — any stock-marketplace asset we license would need an added protection layer (bundling, obfuscation, server-side-only geometry, or a custom encrypted loader) to stay compliant, not just "we bought the right license tier."
- The CC-BY-SA family (BodyParts3D → Z-Anatomy → the two downstream GitHub brain projects) is the most promising free lineage for brainstem/cranial-nerve/thalamic-nuclei detail, but every link in that chain carries share-alike: anything we build directly on top of that mesh data would need to stay CC BY-SA (or be kept strictly separate from proprietary parts of our scene/pipeline). This is the one legal trade-off worth flagging clearly to the team before adopting it.
- The clearest **free and commercially unencumbered** find is the Sketchfab "Cranial Nerve Nuclei" model under plain CC-BY (attribution only, no share-alike) — but it's a single community-uploaded model of unknown anatomical rigor, and Sketchfab per-model licences are uploader-set and should be re-verified at the moment of download, not assumed stable.

## Conclusion

No vendor surveyed offers an off-the-shelf, nuclei-segmented brainstem + cranial-nerve-nuclei + thalamic-nuclei + spinal-cord-by-segment + peripheral-ganglia model that is both (a) anatomically detailed enough to plug the stated gap and (b) unambiguously cleared for redistribution inside a commercial, possibly-paid, interactive web app with no further negotiation. Every path forward requires either a trade-off (share-alike licensing) or added engineering (anti-extraction protection) or direct vendor negotiation (no public self-serve commercial-redistribution terms found). The three most workable options are:

1. **Z-Anatomy (and its CC BY-SA 4.0 derivatives, e.g. the itayinbarr/brainproject GLB assets)** — free, and the only option confirmed to already include brainstem deep nuclei, diencephalon, and all twelve cranial nerves at a usable mesh level; the itayinbarr project goes further with CIT168-registered thalamic nuclei and basal ganglia. Cost: CC BY-SA share-alike obligation on anything built directly from it. This is the best near-term fit for closing the brainstem-nuclei/thalamic-nuclei gap, pending our own inspection of mesh quality (not yet done — recommend pulling the `.blend`/`.glb` and loading it in our pipeline before committing).

2. **Sketchfab "Cranial Nerve Nuclei" (CC-BY, Medical.Gallery Graphics)** — free, attribution-only, no share-alike; narrowly targeted at exactly one of our stated gaps (cranial nerve nuclei). Cost: single-model risk (unknown anatomical rigor/provenance, licence is uploader-set and must be re-checked at download), and it doesn't address thalamus/spinal cord/ganglia at all.

3. **TurboSquid/CGTrader paid stock models (e.g. CGTrader "Human Brain Nuclei," TurboSquid's $29 brain model)** — cheap ($29–$69 range seen) and legally permit web-app embedding under both platforms' standard royalty-free licences, but (a) require us to implement a non-extractable delivery pipeline to stay compliant, and (b) the specific nuclei-segmentation depth of these particular listings is unconfirmed — would need direct product-page/preview inspection before relying on them for brainstem/thalamic nuclei specifically rather than gross brain regions.

None of Visible Body / Complete Anatomy / Kenhub / Primal Pictures / Elsevier 3D4Medical show any public program for licensing raw 3D assets to third-party apps (Kenhub's terms explicitly forbid third-party sublicensing and redistribution of modified content); BioDigital Human is the one "big platform" vendor whose business model is designed for exactly our use case (embed-via-API), but its detail level, true embedding/highlight-control flexibility, and pricing for that tier were not confirmed and need a direct sales/docs follow-up.
